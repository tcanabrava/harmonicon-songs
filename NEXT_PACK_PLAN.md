## Forró scope

FR01–FR06 are independently written baião/xote treatments of traditional melodies, not historical forró standards. Balaio also belongs to southern Brazilian traditional dance repertoire; Boi da Cara Preta is a lullaby. Their original genres must remain in the source notes. If the requirement is specifically recognizable authored forró hits, this traditional-melody route does not satisfy it: those titles need individual licenses before replacing this selection. No existing recorded arrangement or performer’s master is to be copied.

## Rights work before implementation

The eight classical composers and Stephen Foster died long before the life-plus-70 cutoff; their original compositions are strong public-domain choices. Use historical editions or appropriately licensed new engravings, and respect any attribution/share-alike conditions if using an arrangement. Pachelbel’s chart must identify the ground bass/selected variation rather than claim to reproduce the entire Canon.

Brazilian sources identify anonymous folklore/public-domain titles, but do not license their modern adaptations. Select older underlying tunes and document exact variants. In particular, omit Villa-Lobos’s harmonization of Caranguejo, Tim Rescala’s Boi arrangement, and modern commercial children’s recordings. Mama Lisa supplies a melody-reference lead for A Barata, not a blanket redistribution license.

For the blues/jazz originals: Jones died 1945, Cox 1925, Cannon 1912, Smith 1949, Burris 1946. Their underlying early compositions are promising for Luxembourg/EU and Brazil life-plus-70 terms. Check the original publication/registration for US distribution too; modern lead-sheet publication dates and copyright renewals do not identify whether new material was added. Trouble in Mind’s linked Smithsonian score is a 1936 edition; locate the earlier 1920s composition rather than treating that particular edition as automatically cleared. The Cox page is a modern band’s lead sheet: confirm its original melody against an early publication and exclude any newly arranged material. Bill Bailey’s cover verifies the 1902 attribution but the actual music pages are still needed. John Henry has several traditional melodies; choose and document one rather than transcribing a modern performer’s interpretation.

## Implementation order and acceptance

Start with Ode to Joy, the New World Largo theme, Morning Mood, A Canoa Virou, Boi da Cara Preta, and John Henry. Then complete the remaining classical/traditional melodies; blues and jazz standards follow after their exact historical scores and chromatic pitches are checked.

1. Record an exact source edition, page/bar range, attribution and rights rationale per song.
2. Extract the melody; transpose to the standard C Richter layout. Prefer holes 4–7, half-beat minimum notes, 60–72 BPM, and 30–60 seconds.
3. Preserve recognizable pitches. Select intact diatonic excerpts where a whole tune requires unavailable notes; document omissions, octave changes, and rhythm simplification. Do not silently replace chromatic melody notes.
4. Add a four-beat count-in and breathing gaps. Forró and jazz syncopation can initially stay in independently composed backing.
5. Encode the existing basic chart schema and add SOURCES.md plus offline generator data. Supply charts first; any accompaniment must be independently produced.
6. Run the Rust pack validator, JSON Schema and pitch/timing checks, then physically play and review the chart in the game before calling it beginner-tested.
